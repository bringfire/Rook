# Open Interoperability Convergence Brief

2026-10-25. Agreed direction and proposed next study, following the Rhino–Blender–Unreal qualification and the review of GraphRoots and Speckle. This brief establishes design inputs; implementation choices remain to be qualified.

The aim is to preserve the meaning of design elements and the work attached to them as they move between applications and change over time. GraphRoots and Speckle are open-source foundations to adopt, extend, and contribute to. Their current limitations identify possible contributions. The game asset pipeline provides a demanding proving ground for capabilities that can also serve AEC.

The project owner describes bringfire as Promethean and open, grounded in connecting ideas, sharing, and teaching. Those values establish the purpose of this effort. The proposed engineering expression is reusable code, inspectable contracts, reproducible examples, and explanations that help other people build on the results. Rook supplies practical access to authoring operations and a place to prove the work.

**The shared problem extends beyond geometry exchange.** In a game pipeline, an element may carry authored UVs, paint, collision, placement overrides, and gameplay references. In an AEC workflow, it may carry specifications, analysis associations, quantities, annotations, and review decisions. These require different domain rules, but share questions of identity, provenance, dependency, ownership, and revision. Preserving an association must remain distinct from revalidating its contents: a retained quantity or bake can still be stale.

The existing studies support a shared contract with adapters. The owner's clarification adds an adoption principle: first examine how that contract can grow from the existing projects, through extensions and focused changes. A separate core is justified only where the responsibility spans applications and neither foundation is an appropriate owner. Small contributions should remain useful independently of adopting all of Rook.

Three approaches were considered in the preceding review. Making Speckle the central platform favors evaluated model exchange but leaves computational provenance and authored surface preservation to add. Making GraphRoots central favors computational graphs but leaves execution state, evaluated geometry, and application reconciliation to add. The selected direction combines their contributions through explicit identities and relationships; the next study must determine which code to reuse, which contracts to extend, and which responsibilities belong in adapters.

| Responsibility | Foundation or evidence | Candidate contribution to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computational provenance | GraphRoots models documents, versions, components, ports, wires, and library dependencies. | Associate executions, captured inputs, and evaluated output identities with the computational graph. |
| Evaluated model exchange | Speckle bundles separate source object identity, properties, typed relationships, instances, and geometry. | Represent or reference the additional lineage and surface artifacts, with explicit compatibility behavior. |
| Feature correspondence | The Rhino/GH experiments recover source lineage on a controlled planar fixture. | Generalize the provenance contract and test correspondence across more operations and geometries. |
| Authored surface preservation | The Blender authority and explicit payload retain named channels and authored UV boundaries. | Define an artifact contract for surface intent, correspondence, interpolation boundaries, and dependent bakes. |
| Application reconciliation | Fixed Unreal destinations passed the tested replacements; direct imports exposed pivot and stale-content failures. | Keep native bindings, ownership, accepted revisions, and validation consistent across updates. |

GraphRoots' present importer omits execution-relevant data including slider values, internalized values, and some parameter behavior. Its computational graph cannot yet stand in for a complete executable definition. Speckle's inspected SGEO mesh decoder reads one UV pair per vertex; it does not provide the named dual-channel surface contract qualified here. Both are concrete extension questions, with upstream design and acceptance still to establish. [GraphRoots graph model](https://github.com/graphroots/GraphRootsApi/blob/development/docs/graph-model.md), [import omissions](https://github.com/graphroots/GraphRootsApi/blob/development/docs/importer-ignored-gh-data.md), [Speckle bundle schema](https://github.com/specklesystems/speckle-bundle-spec/blob/main/spec/bundle-spec.sql), [inspected SGEO decoder](https://github.com/specklesystems/speckle-sharp-sdk/blob/994761589c7a4d8a405ccbd3ee02ad3b1f048252/src/Speckle.Sdk/Objects/Utils/SgeoDecoder.cs#L143).

**The minimum shared contract should connect computation, logical entities, source features, authored work, and native bindings.** Stable identity must remain separate from content hashes and application-local identifiers. Relationships need revision context. A Grasshopper component GUID identifies a component; it does not identify every generated part or establish correspondence between changing faces. Split and merge lineage must express multiple parents or children without silently assigning one parent's identity to every output.

Large geometry and surface arrays should remain in artifacts designed for numerical access. A queryable record can hold their identities, revisions, dependencies, ownership, and locations. This permits selective updates without turning every mesh corner into a graph node. Parquet snapshots, a graph index, and a publication record have different jobs; the next design should select their use from actual operations and measurements.

Authored work needs an explicit authority. For the tested pipeline, that includes the saved Blender surface data, named UV roles, material coordinates independent of changing native surface parameters, and seam/interpolation boundaries. Geometry, UV, material, placement, and recipe revisions should invalidate their actual dependents. Unchanged inputs should reuse accepted artifacts. Conflicting edits should be evaluated against the last shared accepted revision and the declared owner.

**The qualification suite is a prospective contribution in its own right.** The latest planar experiment preserved inherited UV work across 18 real Brep cases with reconstructed lineage and boundary-preserving tessellation. Its full gate produced 10 passes, seven new-surface reviews, and one density review. Persistent child identity through later splits and merges remains unqualified. These distinctions belong in any reusable example, alongside the negative controls. [Feature-tracking report](D:/RookPipelineQualification/2026-10-25-F01-FeatureTracking/REPORT.md).

The proposed next study should produce three concrete results:

1. An adoption and extension map tied to specific GraphRoots and Speckle code, identifying reusable implementations, missing fidelity, schema changes, and application-specific responsibilities. Favor focused changes that can be explained and reviewed independently.
2. A minimal contract exercised by repeat split/merge/reopen cases, curved trimmed surfaces, ambiguous correspondence, and downstream Unreal overrides or gameplay references. Reuse the qualified transport and surface authority while testing these new responsibilities.
3. A portable example package with source fixtures, expected results, failure examples, measurements, and a teaching walkthrough. Remove workstation-specific assumptions before presenting it as independently reproducible.

Success means another developer can trace a changed source feature to its affected artifacts, understand why work was preserved or invalidated, and reproduce the result. Agents should query that evidence and invoke bounded executors; numerical correspondence and publication checks remain explicit. A useful next outcome is a modest extension demonstrated thoroughly enough to become a credible upstream contribution.

The established [pipeline research](2026-10-24-rhino-blender-unreal-pipeline-research.md), [surface comparison](2026-10-24-surface-automation-comparison.md), and [qualification plan](2026-10-24-rhino-blender-unreal-qualification-plan.md) remain the game-pipeline baseline. This direction broadens their adoption and contribution strategy while retaining their measured preservation requirements.
