Rook is a plugin that turns your AI assistant into a **collaborator inside Rhino 3D
and Grasshopper**. You describe what you need; Rook does it in your live model —
organizing, setting up, tidying, and answering questions, so you can spend your
time designing.

## A collaborator, not an autopilot

Rook isn't here to do your design for you. It's here to take the work that gets in
the way of designing.

Think about how much of your time in Rhino isn't actually design: naming layers,
sorting objects, cleaning up blocks, laying out reference geometry, hunting for the
right command. That's the work Rook is happiest doing.

> **You:** “Take the objects I've selected and sort them onto layers by type, then
> set out a 5 × 8 array so I can start designing.”
>
> **Rook:** Sorts the selection, builds the array, and hands it back — ready for you.

You stay the designer. Rook sets the table.

## The shift

The old way to use Rhino is to learn it — hundreds of commands and options, built
up over years. With Rook, you talk to an assistant you already use (Claude Desktop,
Codex, or one inside your code editor) and *it* drives Rhino for you. You stay in
the conversation; Rook handles the mechanics.

## What you can hand off

- **Organizing** — “Name these layers sensibly and sort my selection onto them.”
- **Cleanup** — “Find duplicate blocks and purge what's unused.”
- **Setting out** — “Lay out a grid and reference planes for this site.”
- **Inspecting** — “What's in this file, and is anything off?”
- **Grasshopper setup** — “Wire up a sphere with a radius slider so I can build on it.”

And when you *do* want a hand with the design itself, you can ask for that too —
Rook will give you a starting point to refine.

## Why it works

Behind the scenes, Rook draws on a library of real Rhino and Grasshopper knowledge
— how components fit together, which commands do what, and the patterns
experienced users rely on. You get the benefit of that expertise without having to
learn it.

## What you need

- **Rhino 8** on **Windows**
- An **AI assistant that supports MCP** — Claude Desktop, Codex, or an MCP-capable
  assistant in your IDE
- An account/key for whichever AI model you prefer — Claude, GPT, or a local model
